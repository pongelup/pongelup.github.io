Research

Research Interests

Sustainable Development Goals, Stakeholder Management, Non-market Strategy, Mixed Methods, and Global South.

Research Program

My research is organized around three pillars, each tied to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

Poverty and Inequality

I study how firms, nonprofits, and governments shape opportunity in communities marked by poverty and inequality. Much of this work takes place in Brazilian favelas, where I combine fieldwork and interviews with field experiments and administrative data to understand how organizations operate in disadvantaged places and how residents respond. I also study how public interventions in favelas reshape the organizational life of these communities. Throughout, I focus on the stakeholders these organizations affect and on who gains or loses access to economic opportunity.

Climate Change

I study how organizations and their stakeholders protect ecosystems while creating economic value for the communities that depend on them. In the Amazon rainforest, I work with firms, cooperatives, and local communities that manage forest and river resources, such as non-timber forest products and the pirarucu fish. I also study water and the oceans, including who promotes desalination technologies and what footprint desalination leaves on marine environments. I pair satellite and other natural-science data with fieldwork to connect global patterns to the choices organizations make on the ground.

Healthcare and Security

I study how organizations deliver healthcare to the people who are hardest to reach and how insecurity shapes where and how they operate. In sub-Saharan Africa, I examine how technology, such as electronic medical records in HIV/AIDS clinics in Malawi, and the design of stakeholder partnerships, such as roadside clinics along transport corridors, shape the care patients receive; in Brazil, I study how the burden of the Zika epidemic was distributed across racial groups and how the health system responded. On security, I study how terrorism, and the climate conditions related to it, shape where and how multinational corporations operate around the world. This work combines partnerships with clinics and NGOs, large administrative and geospatial records, and qualitative fieldwork.

Working Papers

Poverty and Inequality

Not from Ipanema: Security Interventions and Organizational Life in Brazilian Favelas

in collaboration with Daphne Coelho, Aline Gatignon, and Sérgio Lazzarini.

First round at Organization Science.

We study the Pacifying Police Units (UPPs), a large-scale security program that the state of Rio de Janeiro rolled out across its favelas. Combining interviews with panel data on formal organizations and jobs, we compare favelas that received a UPP with favelas that did not.

Internalizing and Emancipating: A Dialogical Theory of Stakeholder Governance

Working paper (desk edit at Academy of Management Review).

This conceptual paper treats the organization as a nexus of relationships with the stakeholders it affects. Drawing on transaction cost economics, the capability approach, and Paulo Freire's pedagogy, it develops a theory of stakeholder governance built on two levers, internalizing externalities and emancipating stakeholders, connected through dialogue.

Did the #MeToo Movement Change the Face or the Picture of Corporate America?

in collaboration with Michelle A. Borges, Aldo Musacchio, Rodrigo Wagner, and Marcelo Orellana.

Working paper.

We study how large U.S. public companies portrayed and staffed their leadership before and after the #MeToo movement began in October 2017. We combine computer-vision analysis of the images in corporate reports with data on executives and boards in an event-study design.

Climate Change

Leviathan as A Client: Public versus Private Promotion of Desalination Technologies to Address Water Scarcity

in collaboration with Ilze Kivleniece and Sérgio Lazzarini.

Second round at Strategic Management Journal. Best Proposal Award, Strategic Management Society, Cooperative Strategies Interest Group, 2023.

We compare how public and private clients commission desalination plants and which technologies they choose to address water scarcity. Using global data on desalination plants since the 1950s together with historical drought data, we examine these choices across countries with different levels of institutional development.

Multiple Stakeholders, One Purpose: Value Creation and Distribution by Protecting Biodiversity in the Amazon

in collaboration with Elis Regina Monte Feitosa and Maria Sylvia Macchione Saes.

Second round at Academy of Management Perspectives.

We study the community-based management of the pirarucu, a giant Amazonian fish, in two sustainable-use reserves in the Brazilian Amazon, the RDS Mamirauá and the RESEX Médio Juruá. Drawing on fieldwork with community associations and their partners, we examine how value is created and distributed among multiple stakeholders when protecting biodiversity is the shared purpose.

Global Footprint of Desalination in the Oceans

in collaboration with Xinni Wu, Ilze Kivleniece, Sérgio Lazzarini, and Hugo N. Ulloa.

Under review at Nature Sustainability.

We study the environmental footprint of desalination plants on the surrounding oceans worldwide. We link the locations and start dates of desalination plants to satellite measurements of ocean conditions, such as salinity, sea surface temperature, and chlorophyll, in a staggered difference-in-differences design.

Healthcare and Security

Grid-Cells, Terrorism, and Multinational Corporations

in collaboration with Leandro Nardi.

Submitted to Strategic Management Journal.

We map terrorist attacks and multinational subsidiaries onto a worldwide grid of one-degree cells observed every year for almost three decades. Using panel models with spatial corrections, we examine how attacks inside a cell and in its surroundings relate to where multinational corporations locate.

Stakeholder Architecture under Resource Constraints: A Configurational Approach to Healthcare Provision in Sub-Saharan Africa

in collaboration with Aline Gatignon, Julien Clément, and Luk N. Van Wassenhove.

Working paper.

We study North Star Alliance, an NGO that runs roadside clinics offering HIV/AIDS and other health services to truck drivers and other high-risk populations along transport corridors in sub-Saharan Africa. Using qualitative and survey data from its clinics, we apply fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) to compare the configurations of stakeholder relationships behind each clinic's healthcare delivery.

Who Bears the Epidemic?

in collaboration with Carlos Inoue.

Working paper.

We study the Zika epidemic in Brazil and how its burden was distributed across racial groups of mothers and newborns. Using national birth records in an event-study design, we compare white and Black or Brown mothers before and after the outbreak, and across public (SUS) and private care.

The Influence of Climate Change on Terrorism

in collaboration with Leandro Nardi.

Working paper.

We combine a worldwide grid of one-degree cells with yearly measures of water balance, from drought to unusually wet conditions, and records of terrorist attacks over three decades. Using panel models with spatial corrections, we examine how climate conditions in a cell and in its neighborhood relate to terrorism.

Published Papers

International Journals

Derksen, L., McGahan, A. M., & Pongeluppe, L. S. (Forthcoming). The Lifesaving Impact of Electronic Medical Records for HIV Patients. Review of Economics and Statistics. Published online October 2025. PDF DOI

We study the replacement of paper-based patient files with electronic medical records in HIV clinics in Malawi. An event study of clinic records shows that the new systems improved patient retention and reduced AIDS deaths, mainly through efficiency gains in managing patient data.

Xuan, D., Hsieh, M. A., Pongeluppe, L. S., Weaver, M. M., Mann, M. E., Jerolmack, D. J., & Ulloa, H. N. (2026). Climate extremes and urbanization drive flood tipping points at the city-river interface. npj Natural Hazards. 3, Article 20. PDF DOI

Using Hurricane Ida's 2021 flood of the Schuylkill River in Philadelphia, we build a street-resolving flood model that combines LiDAR terrain, river bathymetry, and land use across the watershed. Scenario simulations then show how extreme rainfall, tides, and sea-level rise push the city toward a flood tipping point.

Pongeluppe, L. S. (2026). Timothy Kuhn. What Do Corporations Want? Communicative Capitalism, Corporate Purpose, and a New Theory of the Firm. Administrative Science Quarterly. 71(2). Book Review. PDF DOI

A review of Timothy Kuhn's book, which develops a communication-based theory of the firm in which corporations are not unitary actors but shifting constellations of people and practices. The review discusses what this view offers to organizational researchers and strategic management scholars.

Etzion, D., Baudoin, L., Pongeluppe, L. S., Callery, P. J., Lancaster, C. A., & Panwar, R. (2026). Good Data, Good Questions: Leveraging Comprehensive, Direct-Observation Data Sets for Impactful Research on Organizations and the Environment. Organization & Environment. 39(1): 112-139. PDF DOI

Research on organizations and the natural environment often relies on self-reported corporate data of uncertain accuracy. This essay identifies accessible, high-accuracy data sets from the natural sciences, such as satellite observations, forensic genetics, and hydrological maps, and sets out roadmaps and research questions for using them.

Lazzarini, S. G., Boehe, D. M., Pongeluppe, L. S., & Cook, M. L. (2026). The Normative Core of Relational Stakeholder Strategies: Explaining Open Buyer-Supplier Relations in the Context of the Amazon Rainforest. Business Ethics Quarterly. 36(1): 69-111. PDF DOIDistinguished Paper Award in Cooperative Strategy at the 80th Academy of Management Annual Meeting.

An in-depth case study of Natura, a Brazilian cosmetics company that sources natural inputs from Amazon communities through supplier relations open to multiple buyers, including competitors. The study shows how a morally informed normative core, shaped by the company's controlling shareholders, sustained this open relational strategy.

Cordeiro, G. S., Arvate, P. R., Story, J., & Pongeluppe, L. S. (2025). Heroes or Villains? Agribusiness Leaders in the Amazon Region. Academy of Management Discoveries. 11(1): 17-38. PDF DOIAcademy of Management Discoveries Best Paper Award 2025 (Runner-up), 2026.

We study mayors of municipalities in the Brazilian Amazon from 2004 to 2016 and compare those with a background in agribusiness to other mayors. A regression discontinuity design on close elections examines new firm creation and deforestation in their municipalities.

Pongeluppe, L. S. (2024). The Allegory of the Favela: The Multifaceted Effects of Socioeconomic Mobility. Administrative Science Quarterly. 69(3): 619-654. PDF DOIWinner Best Conference PhD Paper Prize at the Strategic Management Society 40th Annual Conference. Winner of the Administrative Science Quarterly Dissertation Award 2025.

A randomized controlled trial of a business training program for residents of Brazilian favelas, complemented by quantile regressions, field visits, and interviews. The study shows that training raised participants' income, while their economic progress also exposed them to more favela stigma.

McGahan, A. M., & Pongeluppe, L. S. (2023). There Is No Planet B: Aligning Stakeholder Interests to Preserve the Amazon Rainforest. Management Science. 69(12): 7860-7881. PDF DOIWilliam F. Glueck Best Paper Award at the 81st Academy of Management Annual Meeting. Best Paper Award at the 13th Alliance for Research on Corporate Sustainability Annual Conference. Best Paper Award at the 17th Annual Social Entrepreneurship Conference. Honorable Mention for the Best Paper Prize at the Strategic Management Society 41st Annual Conference. Responsible Research in Management Award, Micro Area, 2025.

We study how Natura, a Brazilian cosmetics company, sources forest products from rural Amazonian communities, combining fieldwork with satellite data on forest cover and fires across municipalities. A difference-in-differences design, with an instrument based on the missing satellite images Natura used to choose where to enter, ties Natura's entry to forest preservation.

Pongeluppe, L. S. (2022). The Favela Effect: Spatial Inequalities and Firm Strategies in Disadvantaged Urban Communities. Strategic Management Journal. 43(13): 2777-2808. PDF DOIBest Paper Award at the 12th Annual Ivey/Alliance for Research on Corporate Sustainability PhD Sustainability Academy. Responsible Research in Management Award, Macro Area, 2025.

We study how e-commerce firms serve customers inside Brazilian favelas compared with customers just outside their borders. Mixed methods, combining data on product availability and delivery prices with interviews and fieldwork, show why some firms treat favela consumers more equitably than their competitors.

Mesquita, L. F., Saes, M. S. M., Lazzarini, S. G., & Pongeluppe, L. S. (2021). Can trust induce vertical integration? An experimental study of buyer–seller exchanges with distinct competencies and specific investments. Industrial and Corporate Change. 30(3): 778-798. PDF DOI

A laboratory experiment in which players choose between markets and hierarchies when exchanges require specific investments and the parties hold distinct competencies. The study tests whether trust can lead parties toward vertical integration, not only toward market exchange.

Lazzarini, S. G., Pongeluppe, L. S., Ito, N. C., Oliveira, F. D. M., & Ovanessoff, A. (2020). Public Capacity, Plural Forms of Collaboration, and the Performance of Public Initiatives: A Configurational Approach. Journal of Public Administration Research and Theory. 30(4): 579-595. PDF DOI

A comparative study of 24 public initiatives in Brazil, India, and South Africa with high and low performance for their target populations. Fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis identifies the combinations of public capacity, collaborations, and stakeholder orientation behind high performance.

Monteiro, G. F. A., Yeung, L. L. T., Caleman, S. M. Q., & Pongeluppe, L. S. (2019). Indigenous land demarcation conflicts in Brazil: Has the Supreme Court’s decision brought (in)stability? European Journal of Law and Economics. 48(2): 267-290. PDF DOI

We study property rights conflicts between Indigenous peoples and rural farmers in Brazil's Midwest, using cases heard by a regional federal court between 1999 and 2013. Qualitative comparative analysis examines whether a landmark 2009 Supreme Court decision brought stability to how lower-court judges decided these cases.

Narsalay, R., Pongeluppe, L. S., & Light, D. (2015). The Hidden Pitfalls of Inclusive Innovation. Stanford Social Innovation Review. Winter: 47-53. PDF SSIR

Drawing on cases of inclusive innovation in emerging markets, the article examines why new products and services for low-income customers often fail to scale. It outlines the pitfalls business leaders should anticipate and how to avoid them.

Brazilian Journals

Ito, N. C., & Pongeluppe, L. S. (2020). The COVID-19 outbreak and the municipal administration responses: resource munificence, social vulnerability, and the effectiveness of public actions. RAP. Revista de Administração Pública. 54(4): 782-838. PDF DOI

We study the actions that the city halls of 52 Brazilian municipalities took in the first month after their first confirmed COVID-19 case. Fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis identifies the combinations of local conditions and public actions associated with lower transmission in that initial stage.

Monteiro, G. F. A., Caleman, S. M. Q., & Pongeluppe, L. S. (2019). Firm Performance and the Strategic Components of Bankruptcy Reorganization Plans. BAR. Brazilian Administration Review. 16(4): e190072. PDF DOI

We study the bankruptcy reorganization plans that publicly traded companies in Brazil submitted to the courts. Qualitative comparative analysis links the strategic content of these plans, such as the diagnosis of the crisis and the firm's resources, to the firm's later performance.

Pongeluppe, L. S., & Saes, M. S. M. (2014). Ambiente institucional e compra de terras por estrangeiros em países em desenvolvimento. RAUSP Revista de Administração da Universidade de São Paulo. 49(1): 9-17. PDF DOI

Grounded in New Institutional Economics, we study land acquisitions by foreign investors in developing countries. Using secondary data from international organizations, we relate foreign direct investment to investment freedom, property rights, and corruption in the host countries.

Pongeluppe, L. S., Abdul-hak, G. S., & Carvalho, C. E. (2013). O Brasil na Reunião Ministerial de Agricultura da OCDE, Fevereiro de 2010. Pesquisa & Debate. Revista do Programa de Estudos Pós-Graduados em Economia Política, 24(1(43)): 137-151. PDF

We analyze Brazil's position, as an enhanced-engagement observer, at the OECD Agriculture Ministerial Meeting of February 2010. Ranking the main issues in seven thematic dimensions, we use multidimensional scaling to compare Brazil with the other participants.

Book Chapters

Lazzarini, S. G., Cabral, S., Pongeluppe, L. S., Ferreira, L. C. D. M., & Rotondaro, A. (2021). The Best of Both Worlds? Impact Investors and Their Role in the Financial Versus Social Performance Debate. In: Lehner (Ed.), A Research Agenda for Social Finance. Edward Elgar Publishing. DOI

This chapter examines the role of impact investors in the debate over financial versus social performance. It discusses how impact investors can pursue both kinds of returns and sets out questions for research on social finance.

Boehe, D. M., Pongeluppe, L. S., & Lazzarini, S. G. (2014). Natura and the Development of a Sustainable Supply Chain in the Amazon Region. In: Liberman, Garcilazo, and Stal (Eds.), Multinationals in Latin America: Case Studies. Palgrave Macmillan, London. p. 49-71. DOI

A case study of Natura, the Brazilian cosmetics company, and how it built a sustainable supply chain with communities in the Amazon region. The chapter follows the company's sourcing model and its relationships with local suppliers.

Pongeluppe, L. S., Moron, C. R., & Lazzarini, S. G. (2014). The Pulp and Paper Industry: The Case of Klabin Company and Orsa Group. In: Ménard, Saes, Silva, and Raynaud (Eds.), Challenges to Economic Organization: Plural Forms. Atlas, São Paulo. p. 185-199.

A comparative case study of two Brazilian pulp and paper companies, Klabin and Orsa Group, and how they organize their wood supply. The chapter uses the lens of plural forms, in which firms make and buy the same input at the same time.
